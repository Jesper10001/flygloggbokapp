This Privacy Policy explains how the Blades app ("Blades", "the app", "we") handles your information. Blades is a personal flight logbook. There are no user accounts, and your flight data is stored on your device — not on our servers.

1. Who is responsible (Data Controller)

The controller for the processing described here is Jesper Toreld, Upplands Väsby, Sweden. Contact: support@blades-app.com.

2. Data stored on your device

Everything you enter or scan is stored locally on your device, protected by iOS's own device encryption. This includes your flights and drone flights (dates, times, routes/airports, aircraft/drone, roles, times, landings, approaches, remarks), your fleet, digital logbooks, your profile (name, initials, credentials and your signature drawing), what you enter for your Pilot CV (such as education, skills and references), the links between flights and photos in your photo library, drafts, the record of changes to your flights, settings, and any airports you add. Before the app updates its database it also saves a copy of your logbook on your device, and keeps the two latest copies. You can also turn on optional extra encryption of this database (AES-256 via SQLCipher) under Settings → Encryption; its key is generated on your device and kept in the iOS Keychain. We cannot see this data.

3. Device permissions

  • Camera — to photograph paper logbooks, an ACARS flight log, cockpit instruments and aircraft/drones for AI extraction.
  • Photo Library — to pick images for scanning, add fleet photos, and (optionally) match photos to your flights by time and show them in the flight album. Photo matching reads photo metadata (capture time and, where present, location) on your device only; the photos stay in your library and are not copied to us.
  • Add to Photos — to save a share card you choose to keep.
  • Location — to find the closest airport, show weather for the nearest airport, show distance and course to an airport, name temporary landing sites, compute magnetic variation and geotag entries. For weather at the nearest airport the app picks the nearby airports on your device and sends only their codes. Place names are looked up with Apple (see §4). Location is used only while you use these features.
  • Face ID — to unlock the app if you turn on the app lock. This is handled by iOS; we never see your face data.

You can grant or revoke each permission in iOS Settings at any time.

4. Data sent off your device (and why)

Some features transmit data to third parties over encrypted (HTTPS) connections:

  • AI features (logbook, ACARS and instrument scanning, drone flight-log screenshots, aircraft & drone lookup, CSV column mapping, import analysis, building a custom logbook layout) — the image or text you submit is sent to Anthropic (Claude API) through our Cloudflare Workers proxy. The app asks for your permission before it sends anything to AI for the first time, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings. We add no device identifier or other identifier to what is sent to Anthropic; it receives what the photo or file itself contains, which may include names written in it. Anthropic does not use this data to train its models; under its commercial terms it may keep it for a limited period (for example for safety and abuse monitoring) before deleting it. Our proxy does not store the content of your request.
  • Usage telemetry and AI allowance (our Cloudflare proxy) — for each AI request we record a pseudonymous device identifier (a SHA-256 hash of Apple's identifier-for-vendor — not your Apple ID, name or email), the country (from Cloudflare's network, not GPS), the AI model, response time, token counts, cost and status. We use this to keep track of your Blade coin (AI) allowance, enforce fair-use limits, prevent abuse and monitor cost and reliability. Promo-code access, which single-use promo codes have been redeemed with it, your balance of purchased and promo Blade coins and whether a free trial is running are stored against the same identifier.
  • Subscriptions and purchases (RevenueCat) — the app's identifier-for-vendor is used as your customer ID with RevenueCat, which processes your App Store purchase and receipt data to manage and verify Blades Premium, Blades Max, free trials and purchases of extra Blade coins. Our proxy asks RevenueCat whether your device has an active subscription or trial, and RevenueCat notifies our proxy of purchases, renewals and refunds (customer ID, product, transaction ID and period), so that purchased coins can be added or, after a refund, removed. Payment itself is handled by Apple; we never see your card or Apple ID. We do not send RevenueCat your name or email.
  • Crash reports (Sentry) — if the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, a crash report is sent to Sentry so we can find and fix the problem. It contains technical information only: the error and where in the code it happened, your device model, iOS and app version, a random installation identifier created by the crash reporter, and a short trail of technical events before the error (such as which server addresses the app called, without search terms). It contains no logbook content, photos or account details, and Sentry is set up not to store IP addresses. We may use AI (Anthropic) to help us analyse these technical reports.
  • App updates (Expo) — when the app starts, and when you return to it, it asks Expo's update service (EAS Update) whether a newer version of the app's code is available, and downloads it if so. The request contains the app's platform, version and update channel, the ID of the update now installed and a random installation identifier created by the update library; Expo also sees your IP address. No logbook data is sent.
  • Incident news (Premium and Max) — headlines are collected from public aviation news feeds and summarised by AI on our server. Nothing about you is sent to the AI; your pseudonymous identifier is used only to check that you have Premium or Max.
  • iCloud Sync (optional) — if you enable it, a snapshot of your database and your app photos, with a small index file (device name, app version and counts), is stored in your own Apple iCloud account (Apple, not us), protected by Apple's iCloud encryption, so you can restore your logbook on another of your devices. If you have turned on optional extra encryption, the snapshot is also encrypted with your key, and that key is stored in your iCloud Keychain.
  • Weather — METAR/TAF are fetched directly from the NOAA Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov) using airport codes or, for the weather layer on the map, the coordinates of the map area shown. NOAA sees your IP address.
  • Sharing a flight — when you share a flight with your second pilot, the app makes a link or a file that holds that flight's details and your name and initials, and hands it to the iOS share sheet. It goes only where you send it, and not through our servers. The same is true of share cards, CSV and PDF exports and your Pilot CV: they are made on your device and go where you send them.
  • Aircraft and drone images — retrieved from Wikipedia / Wikimedia Commons using the model name. Wikimedia sees your IP address.
  • Maps and place names — maps are shown with Apple Maps, and turning a coordinate into a place name or searching for a place by text is done by Apple. Satellite images on the flight share card come from Esri (World Imagery) through our Cloudflare proxy: Esri sees only the map area requested — not your IP address or any identifier — and our proxy uses your pseudonymous identifier only to rate-limit requests and stores neither the images nor the request. The globe on the dashboard is drawn on your device with the open-source globe.gl library and Earth textures that ship with the app — nothing is downloaded for it.
  • Links to other sites — links in the app, for example to a photo's page on Wikimedia Commons, to our website or to the App Store, open in your browser. Those sites have their own privacy policies.
  • Version check — the app asks Apple's public App Store lookup and our server whether a newer version or an app notice is available (no personal data).

Blades contains no advertising, no ad tracking (no IDFA) and no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs. Subscriptions are handled with RevenueCat's SDK, crash reports with Sentry's SDK and app updates with Expo's update library, as described above. Reading text in photos, cutting out an aircraft from a photo and the scan camera run on your device with Apple's own frameworks.

5. Legal bases (GDPR)

  • Performance of a contract — providing the features you actively use (e.g. AI scanning, subscriptions and purchases, sync, app updates).
  • Legitimate interests — security, allowance and abuse prevention, and keeping the service reliable and affordable (the pseudonymous telemetry and crash reports above).
  • Consent — optional device permissions (camera, photos, location, Face ID), iCloud Sync and the use of AI features, which you can withdraw at any time in iOS Settings or in the app.

6. Sub-processors and third parties

  • Anthropic — AI processing. (anthropic.com/legal/privacy)
  • Cloudflare — proxy, hosting, telemetry. (cloudflare.com/privacypolicy)
  • RevenueCat — subscription and purchase management. (revenuecat.com/privacy)
  • Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) — crash reporting. (sentry.io/privacy)
  • Expo (650 Industries, Inc.) — delivery of app updates. (expo.dev/privacy)
  • Apple — App Store, in-app purchases, iCloud, Maps, geocoding. (apple.com/legal/privacy)
  • NOAA Aviation Weather Center, Wikimedia Foundation, Esri — weather, aircraft images and satellite imagery.

7. International transfers

Anthropic, Cloudflare, RevenueCat, Sentry and Expo may process data in the United States. Transfers rely on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and/or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where applicable. The data transmitted is limited to what a feature requires (see §4).

8. Retention

  • On your device — until you delete it (Settings) or uninstall the app. iCloud snapshots remain in your iCloud until you delete them.
  • Telemetry events (pseudonymous identifier, country, model, timing, tokens) — kept for up to about 3 months.
  • AI allowance counters — monthly Premium and Max counters expire after about 40 days. The counter for the free one-time allowance is kept so that the allowance is not reset.
  • Purchased Blade coins — your balance and the transaction IDs of coin purchases and refunds are kept for as long as the service runs, so that a purchase is never added twice.
  • Subscription and trial status — cached on our proxy for up to 15 minutes, or for a trial until shortly after it ends. RevenueCat keeps subscription records as needed to manage your subscription and as required by law.
  • Promo-code access — kept until it is revoked. For single-use codes we also keep which pseudonymous identifier redeemed the code, so that it cannot be used again.
  • Crash reports — kept by Sentry for up to 90 days.
  • App update requests — kept by Expo according to its own policy; we keep none.
  • AI requests — not stored by us; kept by Anthropic only for the limited period described in §4.

9. Security

Local data is protected by iOS data protection, with optional extra encryption at rest (AES-256 via SQLCipher, with the key in the iOS Keychain); all network traffic uses HTTPS/TLS. We hold no user accounts and no central copy of your flight data.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction and portability, object to processing, and withdraw consent. Because your data lives on your device you can exercise most of these directly: export (CSV, PDF) and delete from Settings, and revoke permissions in iOS Settings. For the pseudonymous telemetry and allowance data, contact us. You may also lodge a complaint with the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se).

11. Our website

We don't set cookies or use analytics on blades-app.com, and the site loads no third-party fonts or scripts. It is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, which processes technical request data such as your IP address to serve and protect the site. To show the prices in your currency, the site asks Cloudflare which country your connection comes from, and for the "Closest airport" example roughly which town (both worked out from your IP address, not from your device's location). The answers only pick the price and an airport near you, and are not stored.

If you use Find airport on the site, the satellite image of the airport is fetched from Esri (World Imagery) through our Cloudflare proxy (api.blades-app.com). Esri sees only the map area requested — not your IP address. Our proxy uses a one-way hash of your IP address only to limit the number of requests, and stores neither the images nor the request. The airport search itself runs in your browser.

If you use the support form, what you write in it, the email address you give, your browser's name and version (its user agent) and the country Cloudflare reports for your connection are sent through our Cloudflare proxy (api.blades-app.com) as an email to our support inbox, and used only to answer you. The proxy uses a one-way hash of your IP address only to limit how many messages can be sent, and stores nothing of the message; it only counts how many support messages arrive each day. Until you send it, the form keeps your draft in your browser's own storage on your device, and removes it once the message is sent.

12. Children

Blades is intended for pilots and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13.

13. Changes and contact

We may update this policy; changes are posted here with a new date. Questions or requests: support@blades-app.com.
